## 1. Chapter Overview
### Why Laws of Motion Matters
If Kinematics teaches us how objects move, Laws of Motion explains why they move. This chapter introduces the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration, providing the foundation for almost every problem in mechanics. From a simple block on a table to rockets launching into space, Newton's Laws govern the motion of all objects.

Mastering this chapter allows students to convert physical situations into mathematical equations using force analysis. It develops analytical thinking through Free Body Diagrams (FBDs), equilibrium conditions, and force resolution, making it one of the most important chapters in Physics.

### Previous Chapter Connection
This chapter directly builds upon Kinematics.
Before applying Newton's Laws, students must already understand:
- Position and displacement
- Velocity and acceleration
- Equations of motion
- Motion graphs
- Vector resolution

Without a clear understanding of acceleration from Kinematics, Newton's Second Law $F=ma$ cannot be applied correctly.

## 8. Common Student Mistakes
[COMMON_MISTAKE]
Mistake: Confusing mass with weight.
Why: Mass is constant, weight depends on gravity (W = mg).
[/COMMON_MISTAKE]

[COMMON_MISTAKE]
Mistake: Forgetting reaction forces in FBDs.
Why: Every contact surface applies a normal reaction.
[/COMMON_MISTAKE]

[COMMON_MISTAKE]
Mistake: Assuming friction always acts opposite to motion.
Why: Friction opposes *impending* relative motion, not necessarily overall motion (e.g., walking forward).
[/COMMON_MISTAKE]

## 9. Exam Strategy
Approach every mechanics problem using this sequence:
1. Visualize the system.
2. Draw the Free Body Diagram.
3. Choose suitable coordinate axes.
4. Resolve all forces.
5. Apply Newton's Second Law.
6. Use constraint equations if multiple bodies are involved.
7. Check units and direction of the final answer.
Never memorize solutions. Learn to identify forces logically—this skill carries forward into almost every mechanics chapter.

## 10. Formula Sheet
[FORMULA title="Newton's Second Law"]
$\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$
**Variables:** $F$ = Net Force, $m$ = mass, $a$ = acceleration, $p$ = momentum
**SI Units:** Newton (N)
**Physical Meaning:** Force is the rate of change of momentum. For constant mass, it provides acceleration.
**When to use:** Whenever a body is accelerating or finding forces in equilibrium (a=0).
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to sum ALL forces into $F_{net}$.
[/FORMULA]

[FORMULA title="Friction"]
$f_k = \mu_k N$ (Kinetic) and $f_s \leq \mu_s N$ (Static)
**Variables:** $f$ = friction force, $\mu$ = coefficient of friction, $N$ = Normal reaction
**SI Units:** Newton (N)
**When to use:** Whenever two rough surfaces are in contact.
**Common Mistake:** Using $f_s = \mu_s N$ when the block is not on the verge of slipping. Static friction is self-adjusting!
[/FORMULA]
